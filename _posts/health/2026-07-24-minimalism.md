Philosophical foundations behind minimalism — useful for understanding *why* a decluttered space feels calm, not just empty.

## 断捨離 — Danshari
**"Refuse, discard, separate"**

The core decluttering philosophy (popularized by Hideko Yamashita). Three parts:

- **断 (Dan)** — Refuse: don't bring unnecessary things into your life
- **捨 (Sha)** — Discard: let go of excess you already own
- **離 (Ri)** — Detach: release the *attachment* to possessions itself

> The deepest part is Ri — it's not about the object, it's about your relationship with "stuff."

---

## もったいない — Mottainai
**A sense of regret over waste**

Encourages using things fully rather than discarding carelessly. It's the opposite of "buy cheap, 
replace often" — instead: buy well, maintain it, use it until it's truly done.

*Example: one quality knife, sharpened regularly, instead of three mediocre ones.*

---

## 侘寂 — Wabi-sabi
**Beauty in imperfection and impermanence**

Finding beauty in things that are aged, worn, or imperfect — a chipped bowl, a scratched table.
This is why minimalist Japanese spaces feel warm, not sterile: a few well-loved objects instead of]
a showroom of new ones.

*Example: keep the chipped mug you love instead of replacing it with a matching new set.*

---

## 間 — Ma
**Meaningful empty space**

Empty space isn't "lacking" — it's *breathing room*, and it's doing something. Applies to rooms, conversations, music, and pauses.

*Example: leaving a counter empty on purpose, not because you haven't filled it yet.*

---

## 簡素 — Kanso
**Simplicity through elimination of clutter and ornamentation**

A core principle of Zen aesthetics — remove what's unnecessary, let what remains be simple and clear.

---

## 静寂 — Seijaku
**Stillness and tranquility**

The quiet, calm quality that results from simplicity and restraint — the emotional payoff of practicing Kanso and Ma.

---

## 自然 — Shizen
**Naturalness**

Avoiding anything that feels forced, overly designed, or artificial. Objects and spaces should feel like they belong, not like they're performing minimalism.

---

## 渋み — Shibumi
**Understated, effortless elegance**

Not flashy — quality that reveals itself the longer you look at or use it. A plain linen shirt cut beautifully, a simple teapot that pours perfectly.

---

## How They Work Together

| Concept | Role in Minimalism |
|---|---|
| Danshari | The *action* — what to remove and why |
| Mottainai | The *restraint* — don't waste, use fully |
| Wabi-sabi | The *feeling* — imperfection is allowed, even beautiful |
| Ma | The *space* — emptiness has purpose |
| Kanso | The *design principle* — simplicity by removing excess |
| Seijaku | The *result* — inner calm |
| Shizen | The *check* — does it feel natural, or forced? |
| Shibumi | The *quality bar* — understated over flashy |

---

*A practical starting exercise: pick 3 "wabi-sabi keepers" in your home — imperfect, well-used things you genuinely love — then apply danshari (why am I attached?) and mottainai (am I using this fully?) to everything else.*
